# Cruise planning rules

## Availability and dates

Cruise programs several years out are released progressively. Use a layered confidence model:

- `공식 일정 확인 가능`: a live cruise-line page exposes bookable inventory for the relevant period.
- `일부 일정 공개`: the season is announced, but the selected month or complete inventory is not confirmed.
- `계절 운항 예상`: inferred from recurring regional seasons; require live verification before booking.

Season-pattern defaults are planning aids, not inventory:

- Eastern Mediterranean: April–October, strongest May–September.
- Western Mediterranean: broad season with strongest choice April–November.
- Alaska: May–September.
- Eastern and Western Caribbean: year-round, with weather disruption risk during Atlantic hurricane season.
- Southeast Asia: strongest November–April; Singapore short cruises may operate more broadly.
- Northeast Asia: strongest March–November, with route changes around weather and port policy.
- Other: treat as route-specific; Northern Europe is usually May–September, Australia/New Zealand often October–April, and Hawaii is broader.

## Budget model

State the currency, party size, cabin assumption, and inclusions. Return a low–high range. Include at least:

1. Base cruise fare.
2. Port taxes and mandatory gratuities when not included in the fare.
3. Round-trip flights from Korea as a separate estimate.
4. At least one pre-cruise hotel night for long-haul departures.
5. Shore excursions.

Do not imply that drinks, specialty dining, Wi-Fi, travel insurance, visas, or transfers are included unless explicitly modeled. Explain that solo occupancy, school holidays, suite categories, and late booking can increase the result materially.

## Booking comparison

Recommend checking at least three channels:

- The cruise line's official site for the baseline fare, cabin control, loyalty benefits, and direct change handling.
- A specialist cruise agency such as CruiseDirect or Vacations To Go for comparison pricing or onboard-credit offers.
- Costco Travel for eligible members when shop-card or bundled value matters.

Never claim one channel is universally cheapest. Compare final payable total, identical cabin category and deck, included perks, onboard credit, deposit/refund terms, agency service fees, and who handles changes.

## Port recommendation quality

Pair every sample itinerary with practical port ideas. Prefer one high-confidence signature activity and one lower-intensity alternative. Flag tender ports, long transfers, strenuous walking, heat, and accessibility constraints when known. Recommend ship-sponsored excursions for tight port calls or high missed-ship risk, while noting that independent tours can be cheaper for straightforward ports.

## Source maintenance

For live or newly dated claims, use cruise-line or booking-channel primary pages. Record the page's reference date in the UI. If sources conflict, favor the operator and downgrade confidence rather than blending them into a false certainty.
